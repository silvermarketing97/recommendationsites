Yes, you can make money online as a beginner with no money, as long as you start with methods that cost nothing to join and accept that the first dollars come slowly. The trick is to stack a few free options in the right order: quick wins first, then skills that pay more over time.

**The short answer:** Start with things that pay quickly and need no skills (selling unused items, a rewards site like Swagbucks, paid research studies), then use those first weeks to build one simple skill you can freelance with. None of these require money upfront. If someone asks you to pay to start, walk away.

## What "no money" really means

Making money online with no money means you don't spend anything to start. You still need three things:

- **A device:** a smartphone works for most beginner methods; a laptop helps for freelancing.
- **An internet connection:** home Wi-Fi or a library connection is fine.
- **Time:** this is your real investment. Free methods trade time for money, especially at the beginning.

You also need a way to get paid. Most platforms below pay through PayPal, bank transfer or gift cards, and opening a PayPal account is free.

One honest point before we start: free methods rarely pay a lot at first. Results vary with your country, profile, skills and the hours you put in. The goal of the first month is to earn something real, learn what works for you and avoid getting scammed.

## How to make money online with no money: the 30-day roadmap

### Week 1: quick cash from what you already have

**Sell things you no longer use.** Clothes, books, games and small electronics can turn into cash within days. Marketplaces such as Vinted are free for sellers: Vinted's help center states that "there are no selling fees on Vinted," and buyers pay the platform's fee at checkout ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.com/help/1653)). Good photos in daylight, honest descriptions and fair prices matter more than anything else.

**Join one rewards site.** Rewards sites pay you in points for surveys, shopping cashback, games, offers and small daily tasks. Swagbucks is the one we recommend for beginners in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and Ireland, because it combines many ways to earn in one account and lets you redeem for PayPal cash or gift cards. Our [Swagbucks review](/swagbucks-review/) explains how it works and how the company makes money.

Spend your first week completing your profile, doing the daily tasks and trying a few surveys. ### Week 2: add paid research and testing

Once you have a rewards habit, add platforms that pay more per minute.

**Academic studies on Prolific.** Prolific connects researchers with participants for surveys and studies. Its help center says researchers "must pay at least £6/$8 per hour," and it recommends £9/$12 per hour or more ([Prolific](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445043-underpaying-studies)). You can cash out to PayPal once you reach the minimum balance in a currency ([Prolific](https://www.prolific.com/participants-how-it-works)). Studies appear unpredictably, so it helps to check a few times a day.

**User testing.** On UserTesting, you record yourself using a website or app while thinking out loud. The company doesn't publish one fixed rate: your test feed shows what each test pays, and payments for completed tests are "usually sent 14 days later" ([UserTesting FAQ](https://www.usertesting.com/faq-tester)). You need a working microphone, and you'll take a short practice test before real ones arrive.

If surveys keep screening you out at this stage, don't panic. It's normal, and our guide on [why you get disqualified from surveys](/why-you-get-disqualified-from-surveys/) explains how to reduce it.

### Week 3: pick one skill you can sell

This is where earning potential starts to grow. Choose **one** service you can already do reasonably well, or learn the basics of for free:

- Writing or proofreading short texts
- Basic graphic design with free tools like Canva
- Data entry and spreadsheet cleanup
- Social media scheduling for small businesses
- Translation, if you speak two languages well
- Simple video editing for short clips

Make two or three sample pieces. They don't need to be for real clients. A rewritten product description, a sample Instagram post or a cleaned-up spreadsheet shows a client what you can do better than any claim on a profile.

### Week 4: land your first small client

Create a free profile on a freelancing marketplace. Upwork says it is "free to join for freelancers," and it charges freelancer service fees of 0% to 15% on standard contracts, depending on the contract ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free)). Basic accounts get 10 free Connects a month to apply for jobs; extra Connects cost $0.15 each. Since you're working with no money, use your free Connects carefully on small, clearly described jobs rather than buying more.

Fiverr is another option where you list fixed-price services. It's free to join, but sellers widely report that the platform keeps 20% of each order ([Fiverr Community](https://community.fiverr.com/public/forum/boards/freelancer-lounge-sry/posts/20-in-2026-is-too-much-actually-it-always-was-tggaq268qe)), so price with that in mind.

You can also skip platforms: tell friends, local shops or community groups what you offer. A single small job for a local business can turn into a reference and repeat work.

## How to avoid scams when you're starting from zero

Beginners with no money are exactly who scammers target, because a "small fee to get started" feels like an investment. The FTC's guidance on [job scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams) is blunt. These are the red flags it highlights:

- **Paying for the promise of a job.** Honest employers don't charge you to get hired. Starter kits, "training" and certifications sold with a job offer are a classic trap.
- **Check-and-refund schemes.** If an "employer" sends you a check, asks you to deposit it and send part of the money back or buy gift cards, it's a scam. The check bounces, and you owe the bank.
- **Reshipping "jobs."** Receiving packages at home and reshipping them is never a real job; the goods are often bought with stolen cards.
- **Big money for little effort.** Offers promising thousands a month working from home with little time are, in the FTC's words, "almost certainly a scam."

A few extra habits keep you safe: use a separate email for sign-ups, never share bank login details, read reviews before you put hours into a platform and keep a simple note of what each site has paid you. If a platform makes you wait endlessly to cash out or keeps raising the threshold, stop using it.

## Realistic expectations

Here is the honest picture. In the first month, most beginners using only free methods earn small amounts, often enough for a bill or a treat rather than rent. Surveys and rewards sites have a ceiling because there are only so many tasks you qualify for each day. Our guide to [making money with online surveys](/how-to-make-money-taking-online-surveys/) explains how much they typically pay.

Freelancing is different. Your first job may pay little, but each finished project gives you a review, a sample and a little more confidence to raise your rates. That's why the roadmap moves from quick wins toward a skill.

A few tips that make the biggest difference:

1. **Track your hourly rate.** Divide what you earned by the minutes you spent. Drop tasks that pay badly.
2. **Cash out early.** Redeem your first small reward as soon as you can. It proves the platform pays and keeps you motivated.
3. **Don't spend your first earnings on "tools."** Keep everything free until you have steady income and a clear reason to upgrade.
4. **Set aside something for taxes.** Platforms like UserTesting don't withhold tax, so income from freelancing and testing may need to be reported where you live.

Not every side hustle has to happen on a screen. Some of the fastest ways to earn extra money are local, hands-on jobs that people in your neighbourhood already need done, and many of them pay the same week.

Here are twelve offline side hustles that work well in the **US, UK, Canada, Australia and Ireland**, plus how to land your first customers.

{% graphic "side-hustle-matrix.svg", "Chart comparing side hustles by start-up cost and how fast you get paid" %}

## Pet and home services

### 1. Dog walking

Busy owners pay for regular midday walks. Start with one or two dogs, be reliable, and send a quick photo after each walk. Reliability is what earns repeat bookings.

### 2. Pet sitting and house sitting

Look after pets, water plants and keep an eye on homes while owners travel. Holiday seasons are especially busy.

### 3. House cleaning

Regular cleaning, deep cleans and end-of-tenancy cleans are always in demand. You can start with supplies you already own and upgrade as you earn.

### 4. Yard work and gardening

Mowing, raking leaves, weeding, hedge trimming and snow clearing in winter. Seasonal work often turns into year-round customers.

<div class="tip">

Offer a small discount for a recurring weekly or fortnightly booking. Predictable work is worth more to you than a one-off job.

</div>

## Family and community

### 5. Babysitting

Parents pay well for trustworthy babysitters, especially in the evenings and at weekends. Check local rules and consider a first-aid course.

### 6. Tutoring

Help students with homework, reading, maths or exam preparation in person at the library or at their home. Parents love reliable tutors and often refer friends.

### 7. Helping older neighbours

Grocery runs, simple tech help, carrying shopping and light chores. It is meaningful work and many families pay for regular help.

## Selling and reselling

### 8. Thrift and garage-sale flipping

Buy underpriced items such as furniture, brand-name clothing, tools or electronics, clean them up and resell them at a profit. Start small until you learn what sells in your area.

### 9. Selling crafts or baked goods at local markets

Farmers' markets, school fairs and craft fairs are great places to test homemade products. Check food-safety and licensing rules for anything edible.

## On the move

### 10. Food and grocery delivery

Delivery apps let you choose your hours and get paid regularly. Factor in fuel, wear on your vehicle and insurance before deciding if it is worth it in your area.

### 11. Moving help and odd jobs

Helping people move, assembling flat-pack furniture or hanging shelves pays well per hour. Word of mouth spreads fast once you have a few happy customers.

### 12. Car washing and detailing

A mobile car-washing service needs only basic equipment to start. Offer packages and visit customers at home or work.

## How to get your first customers

1. **Tell people you know.** Friends, family, coworkers and neighbours are your first customers and your best source of referrals.
2. **Post in local community groups** and neighbourhood apps with a clear description, your area and your availability.
3. **Put up a simple flyer** in local shops, gyms and community boards.
4. **Ask for reviews** from every satisfied customer and share them.
5. **Be the reliable one.** Turning up on time and communicating clearly beats being the cheapest.

<div class="warn">

Check local rules before you start. Some jobs, especially childcare, food, driving and trades, have licensing, insurance or tax requirements that depend on where you live.

</div>
